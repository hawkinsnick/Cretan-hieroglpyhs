# Research ethics and attribution

1. Credit follows each scholarly claim, not merely the bibliography.
2. Published transcription, restoration, dating, sign identification and correspondence remain attributed assertions.
3. Observation, normalization and interpretation are distinct.
4. Responsible disagreements coexist; the database does not resolve them by majority vote.
5. Digital re-encoding does not create an independent epigraphic witness.
6. Cretan Hieroglyphic–Linear A correspondences are hypotheses unless explicitly established at the chosen evidential level; graphical resemblance is not phonetic identity.
7. No universal "word" unit is assumed. Segmentation must identify its source or analytical basis.
8. Rights and licenses travel with derived records. Do not redistribute protected images, drawings, editions or substantial editorial data without a documented basis.
9. Corrections and supersessions remain auditable.
10. Machine assistance is not a scholarly source and may not manufacture readings, citations or missing evidence.
